tight version of the intro sentence:

> The DAT has modest (r ≈ .3–.5) convergent validity against other
> lab divergent-thinking tests in humans \citep{Olson2021NamingCreativity};
> its validity as a measure of machine creativity has not been established.

## The base rate: does AUT itself predict real-world creativity?

Both DAT and PACE/forward-flow are validated against AUT. That pushes
the validity question one level down: how well does AUT itself predict
creative achievement beyond the lab? This matters for us because if the
AUT→achievement link is weak, then "DAT/PACE correlate with AUT" is
much weaker evidence for "DAT/PACE measure creativity" than it sounds.

### Meta-analytic summary

| Meta-analysis               | k studies | N       | DT ↔ creative-achievement r |
|-----------------------------|-----------|---------|------------------------------|
| Kim (2008)                  | 27        | 47,197  | **.216**                     |
| Said-Metwaly et al. (2022)  | 70        | —       | **.18**                      |

So the ceiling of DT→achievement across the whole field is **r ≈ .18–.22**
(~3–5 % shared variance). This is the population-level effect across
decades, scoring methods, and DT tasks; AUT is one of the more popular
but not uniformly the strongest.

### Longitudinal evidence

* **Plucker (1999)** reanalysed Torrance's ~22-year follow-up: childhood
  verbal DT predicted adult creative-achievement indicators *better
  than* childhood IQ (~3× the effect). Often cited as the strongest
  predictive-validity result for DT.
* Important caveats on Plucker:
  * *Verbal* DT predicted adulthood self-reported achievement; *figural*
    DT did not. Plucker himself attributed this to linguistic bias in the
    adult-achievement checklist — a method artefact.
  * Outcome measure was a self-report achievement checklist, not
    objective creative products.

### AUT → objective creative performance (not self-report)

* **Baer (1993), n = 50, 8th-graders.** Used a Torrance Unusual-Uses item
  and scored actual creative products via Consensual Assessment Technique:
  * Poetry writing r = **.08**
  * Story writing r = **.34**
  * Word-problem creation r = **−.19**
  * Equation creation r = **.09**
* **Han (2003), n = 109.** No combination of six DT subtests explained any
  combination of three performance-based assessments. DT tests "completely
  unrelated to actual creative performance."
* **Beaty et al. 2022** SemDis on AUT (13-item short form):
  * SemDis × human AUT-creativity ratings: r = **.351**, 95 % CI [.125,
    .543] (n ≈ 175).
  * SemDis × latent creative-personality factor (openness, creative
    self-efficacy/identity, Biographical Inventory of Creative Behaviors):
    r = **.291** (Study 1 MI), r = **.195** ns (Study 2 FIML), integrated
    r = **.248**, 95 % CI [.103, .393].
  * So AUT semantic distance correlates with a *self-reported* creative-
    personality-and-behaviour composite at r ≈ .25 — but the only
    behaviourally anchored component (BICB: did you write a story in the
    last 12 months, organise an event, etc.) is a frequency-count of
    activities, not creative quality.

### The critic's summary

From Baer (2011, *Psychology of Aesthetics, Creativity, and the Arts*):

> Wallach (1976): "Little if any of that systematic variation [in adult
> creative achievement] is captured by individual differences in ideational
> fluency tests."
>
> Simonton (2007): DT tests "appear to lack convergent and discriminant
> validity. Even worse, they seem to lack predictive validity as well
> because the correlations between test performance and overt creative
> behavior tend to be relatively modest."

Baer's core worry: most AUT validation outcome measures are **self-reported
activity/achievement checklists** (CAQ, BICB, ICAA). The DT task and the
outcome measure both ask "can you list a lot of things?" so the shared
variance may be a response-style artefact rather than creativity content.
When outcomes are *objective creative products* rated by experts (Baer
1993; Han 2003), AUT correlations collapse toward zero.

### Implication for our paper

There are two honest stories we can tell:

1. **Charitable.** DT tests in general (AUT included) correlate with
   creative achievement at **r ≈ .18–.22** in meta-analyses. Modest but
   real. DAT, CDAT, and PACE inherit this ceiling at best, and probably
   less (two layers of correlation: DAT → AUT → achievement ≈
   .35 × .22 ≈ .08 if they multiplied naively).

2. **Skeptical.** AUT→achievement correlations are mostly driven by
   self-report method variance (DT and CAQ both ask "list lots of
   things"). When outcome is objective creative performance, the
   correlation collapses to r ≈ 0 in the studies that looked. Under this
   view, saying "DAT is validated because it correlates with AUT" is
   saying "DAT correlates with a test that correlates with self-report
   checklists" — the real-world-task green check in our Figure 1 may
   be shaky *even for humans*.

Either framing is defensible in a workshop paper. The honest middle
ground — which is what writing_advice.md pushes us toward — is probably:

> Semantic-distance tests for humans are validated against other lab
> divergent-thinking tasks (r ≈ .35–.50), which themselves correlate
> with self-reported creative achievement at a ceiling of r ≈ .2 in
> large meta-analyses \citep{kim2008meta,saidmetwaly2022dt} and
> substantially less when creative output is scored objectively
> \citep{baer2011ttct}. The external-validity chain for these tests
> is therefore modest even in humans; extending the chain to LLMs adds
> a further, untested link.

## PACE / Forward Flow (Beaty, Zeitlen, Baker, Kenett 2021)

Source (NSF PAR, from the link you sent):
<https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10351171>  
Published: *Thinking Skills and Creativity* 41 (2021) 100859.

Note: this is the 2021 **replication/extension** of forward flow, not the
original *Gray et al.* (2019, *American Psychologist*) paper. Gray et al.
introduced forward flow and reported "small-to-medium correlations with
divergent thinking and creative achievement" as preliminary validity
evidence; Beaty et al. (2021) revisit the psychometrics using multiple
semantic spaces and latent-variable modeling, which is why their effects
are larger (latent-level estimates absorb measurement error).

### Convergent / construct validity (two studies, Penn State undergrads)

**Study 1 (n = 151):** latent-variable CFA with forward flow, general
intelligence ($g$), and divergent thinking (AUT-originality) as factors.

| Pair                                      | Latent r    | p       |
|-------------------------------------------|-------------|---------|
| Forward flow ↔ divergent thinking          | **.43**     | < .001  |
| Forward flow ↔ general intelligence ($g$)  | −.14        | .26 ns  |
| $g$ ↔ divergent thinking                   | .29         | .01     |

Structural regression (forward flow + $g$ jointly predicting DT):
* Forward flow β = **.48**, p < .001
* $g$ β = **.36**, p = .002
* 31 % of DT variance explained — **incremental validity**: forward flow
  adds over and above intelligence.

Item-level (not latent) correlations between forward-flow scores and
AUT-originality ratings are much smaller: r ≈ .18–.27 (Fig. 1 of the
paper).

**Study 2 (n = 150), replication:**

| Pair                                      | Latent r    | p       |
|-------------------------------------------|-------------|---------|
| Forward flow ↔ divergent thinking          | **.49**     | .007    |
| $g$ ↔ divergent thinking                   | .52         | < .001  |
| Forward flow ↔ $g$                         | .15         | .313 ns |

Structural regression: forward flow β = **.42**, p = .017; $g$ β = **.46**,
p < .001 — replicates Study 1, including the incremental-validity story.

Item-level r's (Fig. 4): forward flow × AUT originality r ≈ .09–.25.

### Reliability

Cronbach α across the three forward-flow prompts (bear, candle, table):

| Semantic space | α (Study 1) | α (Study 2) |
|----------------|-------------|-------------|
| glove          | .43         | .53         |
| en100k         | .52         | .47         |
| tasa           | .62         | .46         |
| tasa7          | .50         | .46         |
| rep            | .52         | .45         |
| subs           | .63         | .46         |
| baroni         | .63         | .49         |
| **mean**       | **.56**     | **.48**     |

Reliability is modest — the authors flag this as a limitation and note
that more prompts / items would likely help.

### Not validated against in this paper

Creative Achievement Questionnaire, Creative Behaviors Inventory,
publication counts / patents, performance on any real-world creative task
(writing, music, humor, ideation in practice). Gray et al. (2019) claim
small-to-medium correlations with creative achievement; if we want those
numbers we need to pull that paper separately.

### Implication for Figure 1 / intro

The forward-flow "✓" (and by inheritance, the PACE-inspired "✓" for
humans) rests on the same general shape as the DAT:

* Modest-to-moderate correlations with other lab divergent-thinking
  tests (latent r ≈ .43–.49; item-level r ≈ .18–.27).
* Unique variance above $g$ (β ≈ .42–.48) — genuine incremental validity.
* Reliability α ≈ .48–.56 across three prompts.
* No direct validation against real-world creative products.

The honest sentence if we want to tighten the intro:

> Forward flow --- the associative-chain metric PACE is based on ---
> has moderate latent-level convergent validity with divergent-thinking
> ratings in humans (r ≈ .43–.49) and incremental validity above general
> intelligence \citep{Beaty2021ForwardThinking}; its external validity as
> a measure of \emph{machine} creativity has not been established.
